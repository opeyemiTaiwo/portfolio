Research

Trustworthy multimodal AI for high-stakes decisions

I study how AI systems that combine language and video analyze forensic footage and follow specific people across crowded scenes, even when they are briefly hidden from view.

I focus on how, when, and why these models get things wrong, so they can be made more trustworthy before anyone relies on them.

Research

  • 01/2024 – Now

    AI Researcher

    DEPA Lab, Center for Equitable AI & Machine Learning Systems (CEAMLS), Morgan State University, Baltimore, MD

    • Led a hallucination study of three frontier multimodal LLMs (Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro) on 807 UCF-Crime videos across 8 prompting strategies, producing 19,361 forensic reports (21.8M words) labelled for six hallucination types by a cross-judging LLM panel with human validation (IEEE ICDM 2026).
    • Showed that hallucination type is a stable model-family signature (model identity explains 4.6× the variance of prompting, two-way ANOVA), that prompting redistributes errors rather than removing them, and that the most thorough-looking reports are the least faithful.
    • Built HALT, a held-out-source benchmark showing hallucination detectors largely recognize the generating model rather than the error: a text-free base-rate predictor captures 70–73% of fabrication signal across four detector families (under review, ICLR 2027).
    • Showed that prompting-strategy rankings invert across model families and that verbalized confidence is anticorrelated with correctness in two of three models, while sampling agreement is informative (REACH-AI at CIKM 2026).
    • Benchmarked 8 prompting strategies (zero-shot, chain-of-thought, ReAct, meta-prompting, sequential, least-to-most, iterative refinement, self-consistency) with the Claude, Gemini and GPT APIs, using a 10+ dimension forensic rubric with accuracy, precision, recall and F1 (IEEE ICMI 2026).
    • Built an auditable multi-object tracker (detection, Kalman filtering, OSNet ReID) that logs every identity decision; a released verifier re-derives 99.9% of 12,013 logged events. 60.8 HOTA / 75.9 IDF1 on the MOT17 test server (under review, MDPI Information).
    • Evaluated BoT-SORT, SMILEtrack and Track-Anything on a custom multi-camera dataset for re-identification under illumination changes and partial or full occlusion, supporting assistive mobility (wheelchair–drone coordination) and surveillance applications.
    • Developing long-term, cloth-changing person re-identification across cameras and days on MEVID, targeting CVPR 2027.
    • Designed and deployed the DEPA Lab website with HTML, CSS, JavaScript and React on GitHub Pages to showcase lab research and publications.
    • Published and presented findings that give forensic investigators and AI researchers evidence-based guidance for legally sensitive contexts.
  • 01/2022 – 12/2023

    Graduate Research Assistant (IBM Masters Fellowship)

    Morgan State University, Baltimore, MD

    • Developed an explainable stacking ensemble of a Vision Transformer and six CNNs (ResNet, DenseNet, EfficientNet, MobileNet, VGG19, custom CNN) for early Alzheimer's detection on 21,976 MRI images: 98.0% accuracy with stacking and 97.0% with hard voting. Published in MDPI Algorithms and filed as a USPTO provisional patent.
    • Applied Grad-CAM, LIME and saliency maps to explain each model's decisions, showing accuracy and interpretability can coexist in clinical AI.
