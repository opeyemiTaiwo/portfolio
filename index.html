Research

Trustworthy multimodal AI for high-stakes decisions

I study how AI systems that combine language and video analyze forensic footage and follow specific people across crowded scenes, even when they are briefly hidden from view.

I focus on how, when, and why these models get things wrong, so they can be made more trustworthy before anyone relies on them.

19,361forensic reports from 3 frontier multimodal LLMs, labelled for 6 hallucination types
4.6×more variance explained by model identity than by prompting strategy
99.9%of 12,013 logged identity decisions re-derived by an independent verifier
60.8HOTA on the MOT17 test server, with 75.9 IDF1

Thread 01

Hallucination in multimodal LLMs

807 UCF-Crime videos, 8 prompting strategies, Claude, GPT and Gemini. Hallucination type is a stable model-family signature, and prompting moves errors around rather than removing them.

Thread 02

Detectors that learn the model, not the error

HALT, a held-out-source benchmark: a text-free base-rate predictor captures 70–73% of fabrication signal across four detector families.

Thread 03

Auditable tracking and re-identification

A multi-object tracker that logs every identity decision, and long-term, cloth-changing person re-identification across cameras and days on MEVID.
